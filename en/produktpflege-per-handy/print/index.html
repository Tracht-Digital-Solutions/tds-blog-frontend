Why product upkeep doesn't have to be tied to a desk

People taking in stock are rarely at a computer. What changes when items and stock can be maintained from a phone.

June 16, 2026~3 min readBy Julian Tracht

https://blog.tracht-digital.de/en/produktpflege-per-handy

There is a class of task that gets left undone not because it's difficult but because it happens in the wrong place. Stock upkeep is the textbook case.

Goods arrive at the loading bay, in the storeroom, on the shop floor. The computer is in the office. So what arrived gets written on a slip of paper, and the slip travels — same day at best, in the evening as a rule, sometimes not at all.

That isn't a discipline problem. It's a distance problem.

What the detour actually costs

Three things, and none of them is the time spent typing.

Stock figures are always slightly old. If half a day sits between goods arriving and the entry being made, every answer given in that half day is a guess. For an online shop that means selling what isn't there, or not selling what is.

It gets done twice. Once on the slip, once into the computer. And the second pass is the error-prone one, because it happens without the goods in front of you.

It accumulates. Anything not done immediately becomes a pile, and a pile becomes a task you push to the evening. Tasks pushed to the evening get done badly.

What changes when it happens at the shelf

Recording doesn't get faster — it happens where you're already standing. Instead of two operations in two places there is one. The slip disappears, and with it the second source of error.

There's also something often underrated: you can look things up while talking to somebody. A customer asks about a size, you check instead of saying "I'll call you back". That isn't an efficiency gain you measure in hours, but it's the one people notice.

What makes it work

Mobile capture is only better if it's genuinely usable on the move. Four things decide it:

Large touch targets. Anyone holding a box in one hand doesn't tap precisely.

Few fields per operation. Whatever isn't needed right now gets added later. A capture form with twelve required fields doesn't get filled in at the loading bay.

It has to work with poor reception. Storerooms rarely have full signal. Capture that loses data on a weak connection won't be used a second time.

Search, don't scroll. With two hundred items a list isn't a tool. Search by name and number, ideally with barcode scanning through the camera as well.

What you don't need for it

No app from an app store. For the large majority of businesses a page that works well on a phone and can be added to the home screen is enough. That avoids installation, approval processes and updates on other people's devices — and works the same on Android and iPhone.

No hardware either: the phone already in your pocket has a camera that reads barcodes.

A good first slice

Start with one operation, not the whole inventory system. Usually the right one is goods-in or a stock correction, because both are frequent, short and clearly bounded.

If after two weeks that one operation genuinely happens at the shelf instead of in the office at night, you have the answer to every further question. If it doesn't, you know the problem is the handling rather than the idea — and that is a fixable finding.

Tell me in two sentences how recording works for you today. That usually shows which operation would be the right place to start.
